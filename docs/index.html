So the question was simple

Why can't all your agents work in the same folder, and keep your work up to date for you?

The answer

We don't need more software. We need scaffolding.

The new interface is the agent. What it needs is a structure in its own language: where your work lives, what shape it has, what changed and who changed it, how to undo it. Solo Ops is that structure, as one folder on your computer. Whatever you do with your agents, or outside them, all of them can track it and manage it for you.

The bigger idea · for builders

Software lived on servers because building it was hard.

So we rented it: someone else's server, someone else's database, a login, a seat, a monthly price.

Agents flipped both sides. Building and keeping small software became easy. And software on servers became hard for agents to use: logins, dashboards, rate limits, an API for every vendor, and your own data behind someone else's screen.

The operator of software changed, from a person at a screen to an agent working for a person. Solo Ops is built for the new operator.

It doesn't add another layer on top of the old stack. It simplifies to what agents can do today, and keeps pages for the moments a person wants to look. Sharing between people is coming next, built the same way: nothing reaches you without your yes.

A hosted database→The database is a folderplain JSON you own
A REST API per vendor→The API is a commandnode solo.js add · done · move
An integration marketplace→The integration is a text fileAGENTS.md, read by any agent

Questions

Plain answers.

Is Solo Ops for me if I'm not technical?

Today it fits best if you work with AI agents every day and are comfortable running a simple command, or letting your agent run it for you. If you only use ChatGPT or Claude.ai, it works through copy and paste: open the pages, copy a prompt into your chat, paste the reply back, and you see every change before it lands. Making it easy for everyone, with no terminal at all, is the plan if the project takes off.

Can my agent turn my emails into tasks?

Yes, if your agent is connected to your mail (Gmail in Claude or ChatGPT, for example). Ask it once: "every email I need to reply to becomes a task in Replies, due tomorrow". It reads your inbox through its own connector and fills the board; Solo Ops has no mail code and never sees your password. The same works for your calendar, Notion, Linear and meeting notes.

Where does my data live?

In data/ inside the folder on your computer, as plain JSON. It is never part of the public code, and the backup goes to a private repository or a folder that syncs, which you choose.

Which AI does it work with?

Claude Code, Cursor and Codex work in the folder. Claude.ai and ChatGPT work through copy and paste. A model on your own laptop works when it runs through an agent that can open the folder and run commands.

Does Solo Ops send my work to an AI?

Not on its own. It has no model inside, and your agent comes to the folder when you ask it to. The one exception is one you choose: video transcription through ElevenLabs, where only the audio of that take is sent, and only after you pick it.

Can I bring in what I already have?

Yes. Ask your agent to bring in your open tasks from Notion, Linear, Todoist, Trello or GitHub, or paste a list. Run it again tomorrow and it updates what changed, without duplicates. Meetings come from the calendar your agent is connected to.

Can I use it on my phone, or from WhatsApp?

Not yet. Today the pages run on your computer and install as an app on a Mac or a PC. An app on your phone and WhatsApp and Telegram are next on the list.

Can I send tasks to someone else?

That is coming: you hand a task to someone, their agent puts it on their board, and the status comes back to yours. Nothing reaches anyone's board without their yes.

What if an agent gets something wrong?

Every change is logged with who made it and what was there before. Undo it with one command, or from Home while the app runs. Pasted replies never delete anything.

What do I need on my computer?

A Mac, Windows or Linux computer, a browser, and Node 18 or newer (free, from nodejs.org) for the small server and the commands. Editing video also needs ffmpeg, and your agent can walk you through it with /setup-video.

How is this different from Notion AI or Trello with AI?

Those put one AI inside one app, on their servers. Here every AI you use works on the same files, on your computer, and you keep them.

Is it really free?

Yes. Open source under the MIT license. There is no server of ours to pay for, so there is nothing to charge for. You bring the AI you already use; what that costs depends on your plan with it.
