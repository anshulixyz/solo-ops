Solo Ops

local · file-based · agent-native · MIT

A planning board for one person and their agents. Everything is a plain file on your computer. The agents do most of the work. You steer.

Why this exists

Static files are enough now. A folder of JSON and HTML, opened by double-click, is a complete tool: no account, no server to pay for, nothing to sync, nothing that disappears when a company pivots. Solo Ops is built on that bet.

Most of us already have several agents working for us: one writes, one codes, one researches. Managing them is the hard part, and paying for yet another SaaS to do that managing feels redundant. The boards, the schedule, the inbox are the shared memory your agents and you both read and write. They run the loop; you decide what the loop is for.

You become the one who steers. Standups, weekly reviews, turning a saved link into three tasks and a post, cleaning the board, writing the script: a prompt away, logged, revertable. Your attention goes to the decisions, not the upkeep.

How it works today

  • 01
    Plain files, plain pagesTasks, posts, scripts, notes and inspiration are data/*.json. The pages are static HTML. Open index.html, or run node serve.js and install it as an app.
  • 02
    One contract for every agentAGENTS.md tells Claude Code, Cursor, Codex or any file-capable agent where things are and how to change them. Chat agents (Claude.ai, ChatGPT) get a snapshot in the prompt and answer with commands the app applies itself.
  • 03
    Every change is loggedYours, the CLI's and the agents', with who, when and what, and the previous content. One click reverts. Nothing is deleted from a prompt.
  • 04
    Workspaces are projectsEach one picks its own apps and boards. Home shows all of them at once. Inspiration is one inbox across all of them.
  • 05
    Backups you ownnode solo.js backup keeps data/ as its own private git repo and refuses to push to a public one. doctor checks every file and attachment.

What is coming upcoming

  • →
    Collaborators, through agents, no shared appTomorrow you work with an editor; next month a writer joins for two weeks. Ask your agent to brief them. They install Solo Ops on their laptop, point their own agent at the same private data repo, and the two agents talk through the files: tasks assigned, drafts handed back, questions left as notes. No common UI, no seats, no invites.
  • →
    Pull from the apps you already useWhatever your Claude or ChatGPT is connected to (mail, calendar, docs, analytics, your CRM) can land here as tasks, posts or inspiration. The integration lives in your agent; Solo Ops only needs the result.
  • →
    Telegram/add, /done, /next, and "just send me the link" into the inbox from your phone. Design in docs/telegram.md.
  • →
    On your phone, still localThe same pages served from your laptop on your own wifi, a QR code on Home, installed as an app on the phone. No cloud in between.
  • →
    Briefing packsnode solo.js brief --for editor: a filtered snapshot plus the contract plus the right prompts, ready to hand to a person or their agent.

Upcoming means designed and intended, not shipped. The order follows what people ask for.

Principles

  • ·
    Files over services. If it cannot be opened from a folder, it does not belong here.
  • ·
    Agents do, humans steer. Every feature is reachable as a prompt before it is reachable as a button.
  • ·
    Nothing silent, nothing final. Logged, attributed, revertable. Deletion needs a human.
  • ·
    Private by default. Your data never enters the public repo; backups refuse public remotes.
