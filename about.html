Solo Ops

local · file-based · agent-native · MIT

Your tasks, meetings, posts and drafts in one folder on your own computer, managed for you by the AI you already use. You decide who does what: you, an agent, a human, or a mix.

Why this exists

Every tool today lets you hand a task to an AI. None of them hands the managing to the AI. You still open five apps, add the task, move the card, save the link, paste the plan into a chat every morning, and keep the agents in your head. The AI does the work; you do the admin of the work.

Solo Ops switches it. You decide who does each thing. Everything around that decision is the AI's job: adding the tasks and their steps, researching, saving inspiration, keeping the to-dos and the calendar honest, writing the standup, drafting the replies, saying what is next. And it is one folder instead of ten apps: the solo-founder versions of a board, notes and drafts, a script writer with a teleprompter, a posting scheduler, a calendar, an inbox for ideas and a daily brief, each as small as one person needs, all readable by the same agent.

It started as my own problem. I am a creator, a developer, an AI engineer and a founder at once, and I kept needing one more tool, each a SaaS with a login and a price for a feature used once a day, none of which let the AI in. So I built Solo Ops in phases, one tool at a time, as the need came. These are basic things; your own computer and your own AI can do them. Solo Ops is the bet that plain files are enough. A folder of JSON and HTML, opened by double-click, is a complete tool: no account, no server to pay for, nothing that disappears when a company pivots. The boards, the calendar, the schedule and the inbox are the shared memory you and your agents both read and write. They run the loop; you decide what the loop is for.

You become the one who steers. A standup, a weekly review, a saved link turned into three tasks and a post, your calendar pulled in, a board built from one conversation: each is a prompt away, logged, revertable. Your attention goes to the decisions, not the upkeep.

Who it is for

The solo founder

Ships a product, answers support, chases invoices, and has a life. One board holds all four, and the agent that writes the code also drafts the replies and keeps the board honest.

The creator

A pipeline of videos and letters, sponsors to close, an editor to pay. Scripts, a posting arc and the week's deadlines in one place; the AI drafts titles and replies from what is actually there.

The freelancer

Clients, money and errands on the same board, because that is what a week is. Proposals drafted from the last two, overdue invoices that stay red, the vet next to the kickoff.

Three ways in

Just you

Open the pages. Boards, calendar, schedule, scripts, drafts, inbox. The Guide's Quick tab does the daily moves with no AI at all.

A chat (ChatGPT, Claude.ai)

Type what you want in the Guide's Ask your AI box and Copy for your AI: the copy carries your data and the reply format. Paste the answer back; every change is previewed before it lands.

An agent that runs commands

Claude Code, Cursor, Codex. /menu lists what you can ask; /bring-in brings your open work from Notion, Linear, Todoist or a list, /start-day and /wrap keep it current, and every other prompt is a slash command too. The agent runs node solo.js and the pages follow live.

How it works today

  • 01
    Plain files, plain pagesTasks, posts, scripts, notes and inspiration are data/*.json. The pages are static HTML. Open index.html, or run node serve.js and install it as an app on the Mac or PC.
  • 02
    One contract for every agentAGENTS.md tells any file-capable agent where things are and how to change them, with --as agent --note on every write so you can see and undo what it did. Chat agents get the same rules inside each prompt.
  • 03
    Home answers "what now"Your name on top, every project under it. The Today strip: the meeting in progress or the next one, what is in Doing, overdue and this-week counts, Show chips for all projects or one, a quick add that understands "Call Sam tomorrow 14:00", and Share today: a clean card of the day for any project or all, as an image or text. Tasks in play carry ▶ Start and ✓ Done for any project, so today is picked from everything you have.
  • 04
    A calendar without an integrationTasks carry a time, an end and a deadline; a meeting is a task with a time. The agent fills the week from the calendar it is connected to (Google, Apple, Outlook through Claude or ChatGPT) and re-runs are safe. No OAuth in Solo Ops: the agent is the integration.
  • 05
    The Guide beside every pageIt opens on Ask, which starts with Start here on an empty project (bring in your work, plan a project, fill your calendar) and Start with one after that, each with its slash command when your agent runs commands. Quick (no AI needed). Then Ask your AI: say what you want in plain words, tick what goes along (this task, this board, my schedule, recent changes), copy; the prompts for rituals (standup, plan the week, cut scope, fill my calendar…) sit under it. Reply: paste the whole answer, preview every change, apply. Settings. Every prompt is also a slash command, generated from one source.
  • 06
    Every change is loggedYours, the CLI's and the agents', with who, when and what, and the previous content. Home shows them in plain words; Restore a file puts any file back. Nothing is deleted from a prompt.
  • 07
    Projects, not one big listEach project picks its own apps and boards and has an Overview page. Home shows all of them; Inspiration is one inbox across them.
  • 08
    Backups you own, and why your data is not in this repoThis folder is the code, and the code is public, including any copy of it. Your tasks, posts and drafts stay out of it on purpose and get a private home: node solo.js backup setup makes a private GitHub repo, or backup --to copies into a folder that syncs. A guard refuses commits that carry your content; --daily repeats the backup; doctor checks every file.

What is coming upcoming

The direction is one idea: Solo Ops should be reachable from wherever you and your agents already are, and should take in whatever you already write things down in. Nothing below adds a service in the middle.

  • →
    An MCP server: use it anywhere, with any agentOne local server that speaks the Model Context Protocol, so Claude Desktop, ChatGPT, Cursor, a phone app or whatever comes next can read your board and write to it with the same verbs the CLI has: today, add, done, calendar, share today. Same files, same log, same guard. The Guide's prompts become tools the agent can call without you pasting anything.
  • →
    Your notes on the Mac and on WindowsThe things you already jot down keep landing where you jot them. A watcher for Apple Notes and Reminders on the Mac, Sticky Notes and Notepad on Windows, and any folder of Markdown (Obsidian, iA Writer): new lines become inbox items or tasks, with a link back to the note. Nothing is moved; Solo Ops reads.
  • →
    Pull from the apps you already use, through your AIThe calendar and your tasks (Notion, Linear, Todoist, Trello, GitHub…) work this way today. Next: mail ("put the replies I owe into my to-dos"), docs and meeting notes ("the action items from this call"), analytics ("what did the last three posts do"). The connector lives in your Claude or ChatGPT; Solo Ops only needs the result, and every pull is a logged, revertable change.
  • →
    Collaboration with no API in the middleAn editor or a cofounder installs Solo Ops, points their own agent at the same private data repo, and the two agents update each other on what they have done, through the files: tasks handed over, drafts handed back, questions left as notes. No shared app, no seats, no integration to pay for.
  • →
    Telegram and WhatsApp/add, /done, /next, and "just send me the link" into the inbox from your phone. Design in docs/telegram.md.
  • →
    On your phone, still localThe same pages served from your laptop on your own wifi, a QR code on Home, installed as an app on the phone. No cloud in between.
  • →
    Briefing packs and undo everywherenode solo.js brief --for editor: a filtered snapshot plus the contract plus the right prompts for a person or their agent. And ⌘Z in the browser for every change, yours or the agent's.

Upcoming means designed and intended, not shipped. The order follows what people ask for; the list lives in NEXT.md.

Principles

  • ·
    Files over services. If it cannot be opened from a folder, it does not belong here.
  • ·
    Agents do, humans steer. Every feature is reachable as a prompt before it is reachable as a button.
  • ·
    Nothing silent, nothing final. Logged, attributed, revertable. Deletion needs a human.
  • ·
    Private by default. Your data never enters the public repo; backups refuse public remotes.
  • ·
    Bring your own AI. Any model, any vendor, chat or terminal. Solo Ops never calls one for you.
